# ECEG-4192 — Data Structures

**BSc Electrical Engineering (Computer) · Semester VII · Module: Data Storage and Analysis**

This course builds the toolkit every computer engineer needs: how to store, organize, and manipulate data efficiently. You will learn the classic data structures — arrays, linked lists, stacks, queues, trees, hash tables, and graphs — along with their operations, their complexity, and the trade-offs between them. You will also learn how to analyse an algorithm formally, using Big-O notation.

## What you will learn

<div class="grid cards" markdown>

- :material-ruler: **Analyse algorithms rigorously**

    ---

    Write Big-O bounds for iterative and recursive code. Reason about best, average, and worst cases. Distinguish complexity from actual runtime.

- :material-file-tree: **Implement classic data structures**

    ---

    Build arrays, linked lists, stacks, queues, BSTs, AVL trees, heaps, hash tables, and graph representations from scratch — in Java and Python.

- :material-swap-horizontal: **Choose the right structure**

    ---

    Given an application, pick the structure that gives the best time/space trade-off. Justify the choice with complexity analysis.

- :material-graph-outline: **Apply graph algorithms**

    ---

    Traverse graphs with DFS and BFS. Solve shortest-path and minimum-spanning-tree problems. Recognise when each applies.

</div>

## Chapters

<div class="grid cards" markdown>

- :material-numeric-1-circle: **Introduction & Analysis of Algorithms**

    ---

    What a data structure is, why abstraction matters, and how to express runtime with Big-O notation.

    [:octicons-arrow-right-24: Chapter 1](chapters/01-introduction.md)

- :material-numeric-2-circle: **Arrays**

    ---

    Contiguous memory, indexing arithmetic, insertion and deletion costs, and when arrays beat everything else.

    [:octicons-arrow-right-24: Chapter 2](chapters/02-arrays.md)

- :material-numeric-3-circle: **Linked Lists**

    ---

    Nodes, pointers, singly vs. doubly-linked, circular lists — and the operation costs that differ from arrays.

    [:octicons-arrow-right-24: Chapter 3](chapters/03-linked-lists.md)

- :material-numeric-4-circle: **Stacks and Queues**

    ---

    LIFO and FIFO, array vs. linked implementations, and the algorithmic problems they solve.

    [:octicons-arrow-right-24: Chapter 4](chapters/04-stacks-queues.md)

- :material-numeric-5-circle: **Trees**

    ---

    Binary trees, binary search trees, AVL balance, B+-trees, heaps, and tree traversals.

    [:octicons-arrow-right-24: Chapter 5](chapters/05-trees.md)

- :material-numeric-6-circle: **Hash Tables**

    ---

    Hashing, hash functions, collisions, open addressing, and separate chaining — the fastest lookup structure.

    [:octicons-arrow-right-24: Chapter 6](chapters/06-hash-tables.md)

- :material-numeric-7-circle: **Graphs**

    ---

    Adjacency matrix vs. adjacency list, DFS, BFS, shortest paths, minimum spanning trees.

    [:octicons-arrow-right-24: Chapter 7](chapters/07-graphs.md)

- :material-numeric-8-circle: **Sorting Algorithms**

    ---

    Bubble, insertion, selection, merge, quicksort, heapsort — with complexity and stability trade-offs.

    [:octicons-arrow-right-24: Chapter 8](chapters/08-sorting.md)

</div>
